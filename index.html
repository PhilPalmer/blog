Safety First: Input Screening for Protein Design Tools

bioRxiv preprint, 2026 · Nuclear Threat Initiative

P. Palmer, N. Teran, N. Wheeler, J.M. Yassif

AI protein design tools can produce novel sequences that similarity-based screening misses. BioScreen screens the target instead: a database of 14,541 potentially harmful human proteoforms, classified by risk level, combined with embedding-based screening using the ESM-C protein language model. It reaches F1 > 97% on variants of known targets and tells benign mutations apart from damaging ones where BLASTP does not.

A computational method to design broad-spectrum T cell-inducing vaccines applied to Betacoronaviruses

Cell Reports Methods, 2026

P. Palmer, S. Fedosyuk, S. Parthasarathy, J. Holbrook, C. George, L. O'Reilly, L. Wiegand, G.W. Carnell, J.L. Heeney, S. Vishwanath

Spectravax designs vaccines that account for genetic diversity in both the human host and the pathogen. A single designed nucleocapsid antigen raised immune responses in mice to SARS-CoV, SARS-CoV-2 and MERS-CoV — the three coronaviruses behind every major outbreak since 2002.

A modular customer-screening framework for DNA synthesis companies, built in 48 hours. SplitScreen: Benchmarking obfuscated split-order detection methods

BlueDot Impact, Apart Research & Cambridge Biosecurity Hub, 2026

A. Jones, P. Palmer, P. Garg

DNA synthesis screening works on single oligos, so an adversary can split a hazardous gene across many short fragments that each pass. We built an open-source benchmark for split-order detection: a data generator with configurable obfuscation, six detection methods, and an evaluation harness. Alignment-based methods stayed robust under codon substitution where exact-match methods broke down.
